📜 Daily Poems

Poem I — The morns are meeker than they were –

Emily Dickinson · 🔗 Poetry Foundation

Dickinson tracks autumn’s arrival through softened mornings, browning nuts, plumping berries, and maples pulling on a “gayer scarf” as the fields dress in scarlet. She answers nature’s seasonal costume change by deciding to put on a trinket of her own, lest she seem old-fashioned — a wry, self-aware turn linking vanity to the natural world’s own show of color.

Why today? Written for exactly this pivot of the calendar — the first of September, when mornings genuinely do turn meeker.

Poem II — To Autumn

John Keats · 🔗 Poetry Foundation

Keats’s three stanzas move from harvest abundance through Autumn personified — dozing on a granary floor, watching a cider-press — to a closing hymn of the season’s own sounds: gnats in a wailing choir, lambs bleating, swallows gathering in the sky. Against “Where are the songs of spring?” the poem insists autumn has its own music, distinct from and not lesser than spring’s.

Pairing: Dickinson’s autumn is intimate and domestic; Keats’s is expansive and elemental — together they frame the turn from summer at both scales.

🔗 View at The Met · Gelatin silver print (photomontage)

Part of Stern’s Sueños (Dreams) series made for an Argentine women’s magazine, this photomontage stages a woman’s hands emerging among household lamps and appliances — a quietly feminist surrealism turning readers’ own analyzed dreams into images of domestic life as faintly uncanny.
