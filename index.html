📜 Daily Poems

Poem I — Archaic Torso of Apollo

Rainer Maria Rilke · 🔗 Poetry Foundation

A meditation on a headless, armless classical sculpture that argues fragmentation can intensify rather than diminish artistic power. Rilke describes the stone as inwardly luminous, “like a lamp,” suggesting what’s missing lets the remaining form radiate more forcefully than a complete figure could. The poem moves from quiet appreciation to a startling command, treating the ruined statue as a mirror that confronts the viewer rather than a passive relic.

Why today? A Sunday poem about being addressed by something larger than yourself — fitting alongside verses about surrendering the illusion of control.

Poem II — won’t you celebrate with me

Lucille Clifton · 🔗 poets.org

Clifton describes a life built without a template — shaped, in her words, at the meeting point of “starshine and clay” — and asks the reader to recognize that survival itself, self-authored and unprecedented, is worth celebrating. The tone is intimate and conversational even as it makes a quietly political claim, using plain, lowercase language to invite solidarity rather than pity.

Pairing: Where Rilke finds meaning in what a broken form still radiates, Clifton finds meaning in a life assembled without a model to follow — two different takes on making something whole out of incompleteness.
