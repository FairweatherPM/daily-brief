📜 Daily Poem

Autumn Day

Rainer Maria Rilke · 🔗 Poetry Foundation

Rilke addresses God directly, asking that the last warmth be pressed into the season’s final fruits before the year turns. The poem then pivots hard into a shorter, starker second half: whoever is without a home or companion now will stay that way through the winter, reduced to restless walking and letter-writing as the leaves let go. It reads like ripeness curdling into isolation across a single stanza break.

Why today? Early September sits right at the hinge Rilke is describing — summer’s last heat still present, but the turn toward autumn’s solitude already visible on the horizon.

🎨 Art of the Day

Jules Dupré
“The sky is behind the tree, in the tree, in front of the tree.”
— attributed

Jules Dupré · Landscape with Cattle at Limousin (1837)

🔗 View at The Met · Oil on canvas · Public Domain

Painted after Dupré’s formative trip to England, where he studied Constable’s handling of weather and light, this Barbizon landscape stages a dramatic sky over grazing cattle. Bright clouds give way to darker ones bearing down on the pasture, and that contrast of light and shadow is what set Dupré apart from his Barbizon peers.

Pierre Moreau · Imaginary Architecture with Camel and Figures, after Della Bella (18th century)

🔗 View at The Met · Etching · Public Domain

An 18th-century French printmaker’s capriccio: a fantastical architectural stage-set, borrowed from the Italian etcher Stefano della Bella, populated with camels and small figures wandering among invented ruins. It’s imagined architecture with no real address — pure stagecraft in ink.

📰 US National News & Politics

Supreme Court sides with Republicans on discounted campaign ad rates

The Supreme Court ruled Friday that political parties can access the same discounted “lowest unit charge” TV and radio ad rates as individual candidates when spending is coordinated — a policy that mainly benefits the cash-flush national Republican committees heading into the midterms. Justice Ketanji Brown Jackson was the lone public dissent; FCC Commissioner Anna Gomez warned the ruling could unleash a flood of coordinated dark-money ad spending in the final stretch before November.

Denmark’s largest Viking silver hoard turns up in a backyard

A homeowner digging a garden terrace in Rebild, Denmark, struck a clay pot holding roughly 700 silver objects — bars, bracelets, 47 coins, and a small Thor’s hammer — totaling 18.5 kilograms, nearly three times the previous record hoard.
