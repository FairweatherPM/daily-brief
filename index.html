📜 Daily Poem

I, Too

Langston Hughes · 🔗 Poetry Foundation

The poem answers Whitman’s “I Hear America Singing” from the vantage of the Black speaker sent to eat in the kitchen when company comes. He eats well, grows strong, and laughs — quietly certain that tomorrow he’ll be welcomed at the table and that no one will dare send him away again. The poem closes by widening the claim from personal endurance to a plain assertion of belonging: he too is America.

Why today? Friday’s lane is identity and optimism, and this is Hughes at his most economical — a poem about deferred belonging that still lands as hopeful rather than bitter.

🎨 Art of the Day

Thomas Cole
“The most distinctive, and perhaps the most impressive, characteristic of American scenery is its wildness.”
— Essay on American Scenery (1836)

Thomas Cole · Distant View of Niagara Falls (1830)

🔗 View at Art Institute of Chicago · Oil on wood panel · Public Domain

Cole, the founder of the Hudson River School, paints Niagara from a distance that dwarfs the small figures at its base, turning America’s most famous natural wonder into a meditation on scale and the sublime. The soft haze rising off the falls and the untouched forest framing it embody the “wildness” he later argued was the defining trait of American, as opposed to European, landscape.

André Masson · C’est Les Bottes de 7 Lieues: Cette Phrase “Je me vois” (1926)

🔗 View at Art Institute of Chicago · Illustrated book with four etchings · © artist’s estate

Masson was one of Surrealism’s earliest practitioners of automatic drawing, letting the hand move ahead of conscious intent to surface imagery from the unconscious. This etched plate, from an illustrated book, shows his automatist line at work — loose, associative marks that coalesce into half-legible bodies and objects rather than a planned composition.
