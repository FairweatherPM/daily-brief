📜 Daily Poem

Harlem

Langston Hughes · 🔗 Poetry Foundation

The poem asks, plainly, what happens to a dream that keeps getting put off. Hughes answers in a run of unsettling comparisons — something shriveling like fruit left in the sun, something festering like an untreated wound, something crusting over like forgotten syrup — each image a little more decayed than the last. It closes on a question that turns ominous: whether a postponed dream simply fades, or eventually detonates.

Why today? A Friday close to a week full of headlines about deferred rights and deferred peace — a fitting note to end on before the weekend.

🎨 Art of the Day

Meindert Hobbema · The Watermill with the Great Red Roof (c. 1665)

🔗 View at Art Institute of Chicago · Oil on canvas · Public Domain

A textbook Dutch Golden Age landscape from Ruisdael’s most famous pupil: a timber watermill under its steep red-tiled roof, water sluicing beneath, trees catching the light in the loose, atmospheric brushwork that made Hobbema’s reputation a century after his death rather than during his own lifetime.

Giovanni Battista Tiepolo · Rinaldo and the Magus of Ascalon (c. 1742–45)

🔗 View at Art Institute of Chicago · Oil on canvas · Public Domain

A scene from Tasso’s epic Jerusalem Delivered: a wizard conjures a vision to break the enchantment holding the crusader knight Rinaldo captive in Armida’s garden. Tiepolo stages it as pure theatrical sorcery — billowing drapery, supernatural light breaking through cloud — the kind of conjured, visionary landscape that anticipates Surrealism by two centuries.
