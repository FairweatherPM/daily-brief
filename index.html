📜 Daily Poem

Ode to My Socks

Pablo Neruda · 🔗 poets.org

A friend gifts the speaker a pair of hand-knit woolen socks, and they are so gorgeous and soft that he hesitates to put them on at all, imagining them as treasures to be caged like songbirds or kept on display. In the end, appetite for the ordinary wins out: he slides his feet in, and concludes that beauty is doubly beautiful, and goodness doubly good, when it is two warm socks on a winter day.

Why today? Neruda’s Saturday slot pairs well with a slow weekend morning — an ode that finds delight in a small, useful, made-by-hand thing, and quietly argues that the best gifts are the ones you actually use.

🎨 Art of the Day

George Bellows
“I must always paint the snow at least once a year.”
— letter to a friend, January 1914

George Bellows · Love of Winter (1914)

🔗 View at Art Institute of Chicago · Oil on canvas · Public Domain

Bellows wrote to a friend in January 1914 that he missed his favorite snow, and a blizzard hit New York soon after, inspiring this canvas. The bright colors and broad brushstrokes convey the pleasure and motion of skaters and onlookers out enjoying a city winter — Ashcan-school realism at its most exuberant.

Jackson Pollock · Pasiphaë (ca. 1943)

🔗 View at The Met · Oil on canvas · © Pollock-Krasner Foundation / ARS

A dense, mythic canvas from the years before the drip paintings, titled for the Cretan queen who bore the Minotaur. Pollock’s early-1940s work drew on Surrealist automatism and Jungian myth, layering totemic figures and calligraphic marks into a dreamlike, subconscious-driven image.
