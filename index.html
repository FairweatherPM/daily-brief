📜 Daily Poem

After Apple-Picking

Robert Frost · 🔗 Poetry Foundation

A tired orchardist looks back on the harvest from beside a ladder still propped in a tree, a barrel left unfilled and a few apples still unpicked. The scent of the fruit is pulling him toward sleep, and as he drifts off, the physical sensations of the day — the ladder’s sway, the ache in his instep, the rumble of apples pouring into the cellar bin — replay and magnify in his mind. He admits he has had too much of the very harvest he himself desired, and wonders, half-seriously, whether the sleep coming for him is ordinary rest or something closer to a woodchuck’s long hibernation.

Why today? Mid-September in Land O’ Lakes doesn’t carry New England’s chill, but the poem’s real subject — the particular exhaustion of finishing something you worked hard to get, replaying in your mind after the work is done — fits any harvest season, literal or otherwise.

🎨 Art of the Day

Thomas Anshutz · A Rose (1907)

🔗 View at The Met · Oil on canvas · Public Domain

Anshutz, Thomas Eakins’s protégé and later the teacher of Sloan, Luks, and Glackens at the Pennsylvania Academy, painted this quiet portrait late in his career. It shows the plain, close observation of light and surface that Ashcan School realism inherited directly from him, even though this particular canvas stays well clear of the tenement subjects his students would make famous.

Cornelis Floris II · Fantastic Architecture Structure with Strapwork and Two Pergolas and a Small Scene with Vulcan’s Forge at the Top (1557)

🔗 View at The Met · Etching and engraving · Public Domain

Floris was the Antwerp designer whose grotesque and strapwork ornament prints spread across northern Europe’s architecture, furniture, and metalwork for a century. This sheet imagines an impossible architectural folly — pergolas, strapwork, and a vignette of Vulcan’s forge stacked into one fantastical elevation — closer to invented stage scenery than anything ever built.
