📜 Daily Poem

Alone

Maya Angelou · 🔗 Academy of American Poets

The poem opens on a sleepless night, the speaker turning over a hard-won conclusion: no home, no comfort, no peace is truly reachable in isolation. Angelou moves outward from her own restlessness to the millionaires of the world, showing that wealth buys doctors and stagecraft but not company — their families fray and their hearts stay stone. The refrain, “nobody, but nobody, can make it out here alone,” lands harder each time it returns, turning a personal complaint into a flat statement about the species.

Why today? A clean fit for the Monday leadership lane — Ohno’s line about problems and Angelou’s about company both land on the same idea: nothing worth doing gets done solo.

🎨 Art of the Day

Mary Cassatt
“I think that if you shake the tree, you ought to be around when the fruit falls to pick it up.”
— Cassatt and Her Circle: Selected Letters (1984)

Mary Cassatt · Lady at the Tea Table (1883–85)

🔗 View at The Met · Oil on canvas · Public Domain

A portrait of Mary Dickinson Riddle, a cousin of Cassatt’s mother, presiding over a gilded Canton tea service that was a gift to the family. Cassatt’s Impressionist sketch-like brushwork and compressed space give the domestic ritual an intimacy the sitter’s own family reportedly didn’t appreciate — they rejected the finished portrait, and it stayed with the artist until her friend Louisine Havemeyer persuaded her to give it to the Met.

Kay Sage · Tomorrow is Never (1955)

🔗 View at The Met · Oil on canvas · © Estate of Kay Sage / Artists Rights Society (ARS), New York

One of Sage’s last large canvases, painted after a five-month break from painting following the death of her husband, fellow Surrealist Yves Tanguy. Rendered in somber grays, it combines the architectural scaffolding and latticework motifs that recur through her later work — a barren, meticulously rendered psychological landscape rather than a literal place.
