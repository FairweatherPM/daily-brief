📜 Daily Poem

Caged Bird

Maya Angelou · 🔗 Poetry Foundation

Angelou sets two birds side by side in alternating stanzas: one moves freely on currents of wind and light, claiming the sky as its own, while the other beats against the bars of a cage it never chose, its wings clipped and feet bound. Where the free bird simply enjoys its world, the caged bird turns confinement into sound — its song becomes the one freedom no cage can take from it.

Why today? A fitting Monday pairing with Drucker’s point above that we can only ever truly manage ourselves — Angelou’s caged bird masters the one thing still within reach: its own voice.

🎨 Art of the Day

Wifredo Lam
“There’s a moment in painting when everything must be stalked; either the work will be killed, or it will be born.”
— via TheArtStory.org

Frank Duveneck · J. Frank Currier (1876)

🔗 View at Art Institute of Chicago · Oil on canvas · Public Domain

Duveneck’s portrait of fellow painter J. Frank Currier is a showpiece of the “Duveneck boys” Munich School style — dark ground, loaded brush, and a refusal to prettify the sitter. It’s American Realism at its most direct: paint applied like it’s being built, not blended.

Wifredo Lam · Ladder to the Light (1951)

🔗 View at Art Institute of Chicago · Oil on linen · © Wifredo Lam estate

Lam fuses Cuban Santería iconography with the vocabulary of European Surrealism — figures that are part human, part totem, climbing toward a source of light that stays just out of frame. It’s the defining canvas of his mature style, painted after his return to Cuba reshaped how he saw both traditions.
