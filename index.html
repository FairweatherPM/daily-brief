📜 Daily Poem

October

Robert Frost · 🔗 Poetry Foundation

Frost addresses an October morning directly, asking it to slow its pace as ripening leaves, frost-touched grapevines, and lingering mist signal the season’s quiet turning. Beneath the gentle, almost pleading tone runs a real urgency — a wish to hold on to the grapes, and the light, before the coming wind takes them. It’s a poem about time’s fleetness dressed in the calm, unhurried voice of someone who already knows the answer.

Why today? September 3 sits right at that same hinge — summer’s grip loosening, the year quietly turning toward fall — which makes Frost’s plea to an early autumn morning feel exactly on time.

Christoffer Wilhelm Eckersberg · The Cloisters, San Lorenzo fuori le mura (1824)

🔗 View at Art Institute of Chicago · Oil on canvas · Public Domain

The Danish Golden Age painter renders a quiet Roman cloister with his signature clarity — raking sunlight, cool stone arches, and a perspective so precise it borders on architectural drawing. It’s a study in stillness: a nineteenth-century interior view built entirely from light and geometry.

Odilon Redon · Dream Vision (c. 1880)

🔗 View at Art Institute of Chicago · Etching and drypoint · Public Domain

A hazy, half-formed figure emerges from darkness in this early etching by the Symbolist master of the dream image. Redon worked in black-and-white almost exclusively during this period, treating shadow as the raw material of suggestion rather than description.
