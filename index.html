📜 Daily Poem

October

Robert Frost · 🔗 Poetry Foundation

Frost speaks directly to an autumn morning, asking it to hold still rather than let a hard wind strip the trees all at once. He pictures the season releasing its leaves gradually — one at dawn, another at noon — so the change feels earned rather than sudden. Underneath the gentle, almost pleading tone is a clear-eyed awareness of transience: the vineyard’s frost-touched grapes need only a little more mild weather to be saved.

Why today? A fitting note for the turn toward fall — a request to slow down an ending long enough to make it last.

🎨 Art of the Day

John Sloan
“The idea of taking up art as a calling, a profession, is a mirage. Art enriches life… But to make a living at it — that idea is incompatible with making art.”
— The Gist of Art (1939)

John Sloan · Renganeschi’s Saturday Night (1912)

🔗 View at Art Institute of Chicago · Oil on canvas · © the artist’s estate

A founding member of the Ashcan School, Sloan paints a private dining room at a modest Italian restaurant on a Saturday night, catching diners mid-conversation under gaslight rather than posing them. It’s the school’s signature move: dignified, unglamorous city life taken seriously as subject matter.

Qiu Xiaofei · Belovezhskaya Forest (2019–21)

🔗 View at The Metropolitan Museum of Art · Oil on linen · © the artist

The Beijing-based painter builds a dense, half-remembered forest interior where solid form keeps dissolving into color and atmosphere — landscape treated less as a place than as a layered memory. It’s a contemporary heir to the magic-realist instinct: the ordinary rendered just strangely enough to feel dreamed rather than seen.

🔬 Science & Discoveries

Researchers pinpoint a hidden cause of age-related muscle weakness

A University of Missouri-led team found that a nerve-muscle communication protein, NaV1.4, declines with age, weakening how muscles respond to nerve signals — and that inhibiting a second protein, ClC-1, partially reversed the effect in animal models. The international study appears in the Journal of Clinical Investigation.

🔗 ScienceDaily

Amateur astronomer stumbles onto a 390-million-year-old impact crater

While scouting a Quebec camping spot on satellite maps, Joël Lapointe spotted a circular feature that turned out to be a 25-kilometer-wide impact crater — the largest found since 2018’s Hiawatha structure. Geologist Gordon Osinski’s team confirmed it by wading through swamp to find shatter cones, rock-level proof of an ancient impact.

🔗 ScienceDaily

🧠 Psychology & Behavior

Why September “fresh starts” fade — and how to make them stick

A Wharton study of nearly 12,000 gym members found attendance spikes at temporal landmarks like new months because they create psychological distance from a “past self.” The piece argues resolutions collapse when self-encouragement curdles into self-criticism, and suggests an Internal Family Systems approach — listening to competing internal “parts” instead of overriding them — holds up better than willpower alone.

🔗 Psychology Today

Passive scrolling through news hits mental health harder than engaging with it

A large study of social-media users found that consuming news passively — scrolling headlines, bookmarking, never commenting — was linked to more stress, anxiety and depressive language than actively discussing it. News engagement overall still correlated with less loneliness, suggesting the harm comes from the scroll itself, not the news.

🔗 Psychology Today
