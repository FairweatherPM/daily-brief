📜 Daily Poem

Besides the Autumn poets sing (131)

Emily Dickinson · 🔗 Academy of American Poets

Dickinson argues that autumn has its own quiet catalog of days that the famous “Autumn poets” never bother to sing about — the plain, in-between mornings that sit past goldenrod season and short of the first snow. She catalogs small, subdued sensations: a stillness settling over the brook, valves of scent sealing shut, a squirrel who might stay on as company. It closes on a plea for a “sunny mind” to meet whatever the season’s “windy will” brings, turning a nature poem into a quiet request for equanimity.

Why today? Mid-September in Land O’ Lakes still runs warm, but the light and mood are starting their long turn — exactly the “prosaic,” in-between days this poem is about.

🎨 Art of the Day

Leonora Carrington
“Painting is my vehicle of transit. I don’t always know where I am going or what it means.”
— attributed

Anselm Feuerbach · Ricordo di Tivoli (Remembrance of Tivoli) (1866–67)

🔗 View at Alte Nationalgalerie, Berlin · Oil on canvas · Public Domain

Feuerbach painted two Italian peasant children resting near a waterfall outside Rome, idealizing them through costume — the girl in near-contemporary dress but archaic jewelry, the boy anachronistically robed like an ancient Roman. It’s one of his “Idylle” pictures from the 1860s, replacing narrative action with mood alone.

Leonora Carrington · Self-Portrait (Inn of the Dawn Horse) (ca. 1937–38)

🔗 View at The Met · Oil on canvas · © Estate of Leonora Carrington / Artists Rights Society (ARS)

Painted at twenty, just after Carrington fled her upper-middle-class English upbringing for Paris, this dreamlike scene shows her perched on a chair, one hand extended toward a prancing hyena, her back to a tailless rocking horse flying out the window behind her. The white horse became her recurring symbolic stand-in throughout her life’s work.

Supreme Court rejects Trump’s mail-ballot restrictions ahead of midterms

🌍 International

NATO fighter jets shoot down drone over Lithuania

Italian Air Force jets flying NATO’s Baltic Air Policing mission destroyed a drone that crossed into Lithuania from Belarus overnight near Pratkūnai village — the first time a drone has been shot down in Lithuanian airspace. It stayed aloft about 30 minutes before interception. The incident came a day after NATO chief Mark Rutte warned that strikes near alliance territory would only deepen NATO’s support for Kyiv.

🏛️ History & Art

Marble slab reveals a Roman lightning ritual at Hadrian’s Villa

Archaeologists excavating near the amphitheater at Hadrian’s Villa in Tivoli found a marble slab, still fixed in its original masonry, inscribed “FVLGVR SVBMANIVM” — “the lightning bolt of Summanus,” the Roman god blamed for nocturnal lightning (as opposed to Jupiter’s daytime bolts). The find is a rare intact example of the fulgur conditum ritual, in which ground struck by lightning was declared sacred and ritually enclosed.
